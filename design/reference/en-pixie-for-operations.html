Pixie for Operations

Decision AI that keeps
operations running.

Pixie for Operations helps critical infrastructure and industrial organizations stay operational under cyberattack. From your asset inventories, network diagrams, contracts, and runbooks, it builds an outage map showing which systems, identities, contractors, and backups your critical operations depend on. It then uses that map to propose containment plans that stop the spread while keeping as much critical business running as possible, explained in terms executives can act on.

Pixie for Operations is in development and is being designed first for organizations operating in Japan. This page describes our current plans, which may change.

EDR, SIEM, vulnerability management, and OT monitoring each watch their own domain. Executives want to know something else: what will stop, and when it will come back. Pixie for Operations connects the two.

Pixie for Operations does not replace your existing tools. It takes in their output and adds a decision layer focused on keeping the business running.

Prepare in advance.
Decide with clarity.

Three functions, the outage map, containment AI, and the recovery check, connect everyday preparation and incident decisions through a single dependency graph. The outage map is the foundation.

1
Map it in advance

Outage map

AI reads your existing documents and tool exports to build the dependency chain from critical operations to systems, identities, contractors, people, and backups.

What it reads
  • Asset inventories (Excel)
  • Network diagrams (images, PDF)
  • Maintenance and outsourcing contracts, contractor lists
  • VPN and remote-access requests
  • BCPs and recovery runbooks
  • Exports from EDR, vulnerability management, IAM, and OT monitoring
What you learn
  • A dependency graph built around your operations
  • Contradictions between documents, such as a maintenance line in a VPN request that is missing from the network diagram
  • Dormant contractor accounts and MFA exceptions
  • Candidate systems for regulatory notification, with the evidence for each
2
Choose in an incident

Containment AI

When an attack hits, it shows what to cut, what will stop, and what will keep running, looking for isolation steps that stop the spread while preserving critical operations. AI proposes the plan and shows its impact; people decide whether to act.

Before
For each possible point of entry, it precomputes isolation plans that stop the spread while preserving as much critical business as possible. Operations that will stop are linked to fallback procedures, such as taking orders by phone or fax, and to the people needed.
During
It reads EDR and SIEM alerts and proposes the prepared plan that fits the situation, explaining it to executives in plain terms: “With this plan, order intake stops, but shipping continues.”
Testing
An attacker AI probes a digital twin for paths around each plan. It never touches production systems.
3
Confirm you can recover (extension)

Recovery check

AI checks on paper whether a system could be restored if it were encrypted, and generates tabletop scenarios tailored to your organization.

Recovery blockers it looks for
  • Runbooks stored on the systems that would go down
  • Recovery accounts that depend on an identity platform that would go down
  • Backups kept in the same environment as the original systems
  • Only one person who knows the procedure
  • Restores that cannot meet the recovery time objective (RTO)

Useful all year,
not just in a crisis.

Security leads use it to maintain the map and plan containment. CSIRT and FSIRT teams use it to make decisions during incidents. Business owners and executives use it to understand impact and recovery time.

An outage map is also
an attacker’s map.

A dependency graph is valuable to attackers, too. That is why Pixie for Operations is designed to keep your configuration data inside your environment.

Read-only

It never writes to your environment and needs no sensors or agents.

Closed networks and on-premises

Choose an LLM setup that runs on a closed network, or run it on-premises.

Least privilege and audit logs

It runs with only the permissions it needs and records actions in audit logs.

Testing off production

Containment plans are tested only on a digital twin.
